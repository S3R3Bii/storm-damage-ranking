# Primary sources and prior work

- [NOAA NCEI Storm Events Database](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/storm-events-database/) and [official CSV file index](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/pub/data/swdi/stormevents/csvfiles/). Exact annual details file names were verified from the index on 29 October 2026; original bulk files remain private and only a minimized report subset will be redistributed.
- [Storm Data bulk format](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/pub/data/swdi/stormevents/csvfiles/Storm-Data-Bulk-csv-Format.pdf). Event IDs are report keys; episodes may contain several events. The five chosen event types are county-designated; K/M dollar fields record estimated property loss. These identifiers are not complete physical-storm lineage.
- [NCEI FAQ](https://www.ncdc.noaa.gov/stormevents/faq.jsp). Damage figures are broad estimates in event-year dollars; tornado segments can cross county/state boundaries. The current snapshot is a later revision, not real-time information.
- [NWS Storm Data Preparation directive](https://www.weather.gov/media/directives/010_pdfs/pd01016005curr.pdf), damage section. Unknown damage and estimated damage are distinct reporting possibilities; flood damage has a special reporting requirement. This supports preserving missing/zero distinctions, not imputing a known true loss.
- [NWS data reuse disclaimer](https://www.weather.gov/disclaimer) and [NCEI open-data policy](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/sites/default/files/2023-12/NCEI%20PD-10-2-02%20-%20Open%20Data%20Policy%20Signed.pdf). NOAA/NWS material is generally public domain with stated exceptions, attribution and no implied endorsement or presentation of modified data as official. Only eligible aggregate statistical fields are retained.
- Renato P. dos Santos (2016), [Some comments on the reliability of NOAA's Storm Events Database](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.06973), arXiv:1606.06973v2. This primary exploratory preprint found missing damage and inconsistent labels in older data. Its results are historical context, not evidence for missingness rates in this study's 2015–2023 snapshot. Abstract, data/method sections and source definitions were reviewed; no source paper is redistributed. The present comparison does not claim that the general incompleteness problem is new.

The contribution to test is the scoped ranking comparison and resampling-unit sensitivity, not a new bootstrap method, complete literature gap or estimate of actual underreporting. Scenario quantiles and caps are prespecified sensitivity conventions, with empirical values calibrated only on the earlier period.
